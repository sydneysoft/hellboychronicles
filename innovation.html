StoryLingo · Product & Innovation Record

READING FIRST.
LANGUAGE LEARNING INSIDE THE STORY.

StoryLingo is a multilingual reading product designed around a simple idea: a learner should be able to stay inside a story while comparing languages, practising vocabulary and moving between different reading formats.

What the product is building

STORY-ALIGNED MULTILINGUAL READING

The same narrative can be represented across multiple languages, allowing readers to revisit equivalent scenes and phrases without changing learning products.

READING-FIRST LEARNING UX

Language support is attached to the reading experience instead of being limited to isolated lesson screens.

MULTIPLE READING MODES

Illustrated, graphic and text editions can coexist in the same library so learners can choose a reading format that matches how they want to engage with the language.

CROSS-CULTURAL CONTENT SYSTEM

The library is structured to hold collections from different language traditions alongside original StoryLingo material and shared-language stories.

Current product architecture

StoryLingo uses a static-first web architecture deployed on Vercel, with lightweight serverless endpoints for security-related functions. This keeps the reading experience fast while allowing individual language, reader and collection experiences to evolve independently.

The production library currently contains multilingual and language-specific material, including Ukrainian, Polish, French, German and Russian collections plus a shared-language edition of The Lost Key.

Measuring real use

From 17 September 2026, StoryLingo began instrumenting aggregate product usage so development decisions can be supported by measurable reader behaviour.

Tracked product signals include story openings, language changes, practice starts and audio starts, alongside aggregate page views, visitors, routes, referrers and country distribution provided by the production analytics platform.

Analytics metadata is intentionally limited to product interaction data such as routes, interface language and event names. Story text or vocabulary entered by a reader is not sent as custom analytics metadata by this instrumentation.

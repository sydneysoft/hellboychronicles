Polish Grammar Guide

Pronouns, article equivalents and common adjectives that appear throughout the Polish folk tales. Learn the form, hear it, then practice typing it.

Pronouns: Polish often drops the subject pronoun because the verb ending already shows who is acting, but these forms are still essential for reading dialogue and emphasis.
Important: Polish has no direct equivalents of English a, an, the. Instead, context usually tells you whether a noun is specific. Words such as ten, ta, to (“this/that”) can add specificity, so this section teaches the closest useful equivalents you will see in the tales.
Adjectives: Polish adjectives change to match the gender, number and case of the noun. The guide shows the basic masculine / feminine / neuter forms most useful for recognizing words in the stories.
